A beer pong tournament is easy to set up: sign up teams, set the cups, start playing. It only gets really exciting when the format, the rules and the setting fit together. This guide walks you through the planning in the order you will need it and shows what to think about.

## 1. Pick the right format

The format decides how long the tournament lasts and how often each team gets to play. Three versions have proven themselves:

- **Group stage plus knockout round:** Every team plays several games, so nobody is out after ten minutes. Afterwards the bracket decides the winner. The best choice for most evenings.
- **Group stage only:** Everyone plays everyone. It is fair and relaxed, but there is no final. Works well with four to six teams.
- **Pure knockout:** Fast and unforgiving, every game counts. Teams that lose can only watch the rest.

An example: eight teams in two groups of four make 12 group games. The top two of each group play the semifinals and the final, three more games, so 15 in total. At roughly 10 to 15 minutes per game, that takes about an hour and a half on two tables. With only one table, plan for about three hours.

> With an odd number of teams in the knockout bracket, the best teams get a bye and join one round later.

## 2. Build fair teams

Games are exciting when the outcome is open. You can get there while forming the teams:

- **Draw them:** Pull the teams out of a hat. It mixes the group, and less experienced players get a real chance.
- **Strong plus weak:** If you know everyone's skill level, pair an experienced player with a less experienced one.
- **Seeded teams:** With fixed teams, spread the strongest ones across different groups so they do not meet in the first round.
- **Team names:** Give every team a name. It sounds minor, but it makes a big difference for announcements and in the standings.

## 3. Set the rules in advance

Most arguments do not happen while throwing, but because of unclear rules. So decide the following before the tournament and send it to everyone:

- how many cups are used (10-cup formation as the classic, 6 cups for shorter games)
- how many re-racks are allowed per game
- how a tie in the standings is settled (head-to-head, cup difference or a tiebreaker game)
- who decides in a dispute, for example a tournament director or a referee

You can use our [beer pong rules](/en/rules) as a starting point.

If you want some variety, house rules help. They just need to be fixed before the first game:

- The final is played with the full 10-cup formation, group games with 6 cups.
- Each group has a trick shot duel, and the winner starts the next game with a one-cup lead.
- Rule violations cost a penalty cup.

## 4. Build up the tension

- **Show the standings and the bracket:** On a TV, a projector or on phones, everyone sees who plays whom and who still has a chance. That creates rivalries.
- **Announce the schedule:** Say before the start in which order the games are played. Teams then know when they are up, and there are no long waits.
- **Make the final the highlight:** Give the final its own time slot. All tables rest, everyone watches, maybe with an announcement and walk-out music.
- **Prediction game:** Everyone picks the winner before the tournament, with no money involved. Whoever is right gets a small prize. That way even eliminated teams stay invested.

## 5. Atmosphere and setting

A tournament lives on atmosphere. A few things that take little effort:

- Someone takes over the **commentary** and announces teams and games.
- A **playlist** sets the mood, and the final can get a song of its own.
- **Photos** of the winning team and of the best shots stay in memory longer than any standings table.
- **Everyone belongs:** If someone does not want to drink, they play with water or soda in the cups. The game counts just the same. Have water ready, plan breaks and sort out the ride home in good time.

## 6. Prizes and awards

Winning needs something you can hold. The value matters less than whether the prize has a story.

![Trophy and winner's ring as an example of tournament prizes](/images/features/pokale-ringe-hero.webp)

- **Challenge trophy:** The winner keeps it until the next tournament, and a name is engraved every year.
- **Winner's ring or medals:** An engraved ring or medals for the top three places are available with custom engraving from many online shops.
- **Special awards:** Best trick shot, fairest team, best outfit, or a fighter's award for the team with the most comebacks.
- **The wooden spoon:** A wink for last place, so everyone laughs at the end.

3. Write down the rules and send them in advance
4. Get cups, balls (with spares) and water
5. Organize prizes and special awards
6. Create the tournament and enter the teams
